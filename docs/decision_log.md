# Decision log

The decisions below are implementation interpretations where the supplied brief leaves room for choice. Alternatives are recorded to make the choice reviewable; “confirmation required” means the behavior must not be presented as organizer-approved policy.

| Decision area | Decision and reason | Alternative considered | Impact | Confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Introductory commission | Include onboarding day 60 because the requirement says through day 60. | End the rate before day 60. | Defines the exact rate boundary. | No; stated by requirement. |
| Financial year/TDS | Reset April 1; exclude canceled GMV; withhold only after prior cumulative GMV exceeds Rs 5 lakh. A sequential ledger makes the convention deterministic. | Withhold on the transaction that crosses the threshold. | Changes TDS at the threshold boundary. | Yes; crossing transaction convention. |
| Discount base and funders | Restaurant discount reduces the base; platform share is reimbursed; co-funding splits with odd paisa to platform. | Deduct all coupons from restaurant earnings or round the platform share down. | Affects commission and restaurant net. | Confirm only if organizer specification differs. |
| Tax and cancellation | Apply stated rates to documented bases; where cancellations/refunds have no specified adjustment, retain the current calculation as an explicit assumption. | Invent a prorated tax reversal or cancellation-specific rate. | Changes GST and cancellation payouts. | Yes; refund/cancellation tax base. |
| Cancellation refund | Model the full cancellation refund from discounted customer item amount plus packaging; apply the stated restaurant penalty only when accepted. | Refund gross items without discount/packaging adjustments. | Changes customer refund and restaurant debit. | Yes; refund base and interactions. |
| Weekly selection | Use Monday through Sunday and choose close-date week for a closed dispute. | Keep the order-date week after a dispute closes. | Determines which statement includes the order. | No for week mapping; Tuesday-only execution still needs confirmation. |
| Ads | Cap requested ads by budget and positive available payout; carry unpaid requested spend. | Allow ads to create a negative payout or expire unpaid authorized spend. | Determines payout and future debit. | No; matches documented rule. |
| Platform fee | Use the stated Rs 10 sub-Rs 100 minimum; accept other supplied fees as precomputed deductions. | Infer a formula or tax from distance/order value. | Avoids inventing an unsupported fee formula. | Yes; general fee formula/tax and cancellation treatment. |
| Delivery fee | Report the supplied amount, exclude from payout until collector/recipient is specified. | Deduct it from restaurant or add it to payout. | Preserves visibility without choosing a beneficiary. | Yes; formula, collector and allocation. |
| Dispute refund schema | Allow one funding reason per order and reject conflicting funders rather than silently allocating them. | Add multiple refund-funder ledgers. | Limits supported cumulative dispute refunds. | Yes; confirm multiple funders requirement. |
| Corrections | Append revisions and per-order audit; update carry only when correcting the latest statement. | Rewrite history or cascade all later statements automatically. | Preserves original evidence but leaves later statements unchanged. | Yes; downstream statement/carry cascade. |
| Duplicate processing | Hash complete file bytes and reject repeated IDs in different files. | Treat each file replay as a new import or silently skip duplicate IDs. | Gives idempotent replay with atomic duplicate protection. | No; supports ledger integrity. |

Technical choices—SQLite for a local single-user ledger, Decimal for money, parameterized SQL, temporary databases in tests, and additive schema migrations—preserve correctness and compatibility without adding dependencies or deleting records.

1. Day 60 after onboarding is included in the 12% introductory rate.
2. FY starts April 1. The order that takes cumulative eligible GMV over Rs 5 lakh is excluded; later eligible orders incur 1% TDS. Eligible GMV excludes canceled orders, includes delivered and partially refunded orders, and is sequenced by order timestamp then ID. Open disputes count toward the GMV threshold, but their own TDS is recorded when settled.
3. Section 9(5) GST is 5% of gross item value for non-canceled orders; canceled orders have zero realized item value and zero GST. Commission GST is 18% of commission. The brief does not explicitly state canceled-order GST treatment.
4. Commission base is gross item value minus restaurant-funded discounts. A platform discount is deducted from the gross proceeds and then added back as a platform reimbursement, so it does not change restaurant earnings or commission base. For a co-funded offer, an odd paise goes to the platform share.
5. Packaging comes from the order when supplied, otherwise the restaurant setting; it is capped at Rs 30. Full cancellation refund is assumed to equal the customer-paid item total after all discounts plus packaging.
6. Payout weeks use Monday through Sunday. For an open dispute, close-date week replaces order-date week; TDS and GMV year remain tied to original order date.
7. Customer cancellation at exactly 60 seconds is free. Accepted restaurant cancellation has Rs 100 penalty; unaccepted cancellation has no penalty. A negative settlement payout is retained to show an amount owed rather than erasing the penalty.
8. TDS excludes the exact order that crosses the threshold, as an exercise convention. The brief does not specify whether withholding starts on the crossing transaction.
9. Ad spend is passed on the weekly command and limited by restaurant budget and positive pre-ad payout. Unpaid requested spend rolls forward; unused weekly budget expires.
10. Order ratings in the preceding Monday–Sunday interval drive following-week commission uplift. A weekly average exactly 3.5 does not trigger it. The recorded weekly average below 3 suspends future imports; a later higher rating does not automatically restore ordering.
11. Under Rs 100 net item value, the commission percentage is replaced by a Rs 10 platform fee; commission and commission GST are zero. This minimum platform fee applies to delivered and partially refunded orders, not canceled orders. The brief does not specify cancellation/minimum-fee interaction or GST on this platform fee.
12. `platform_fee` supplied for orders at or above Rs 100 is treated as an already-calculated restaurant-borne amount and deducted from payout. Under Rs 100 it is overridden by the fixed Rs 10 minimum fee.
13. `delivery_fee` is an explicitly supplied amount, shown per order, in weekly statements and audit records, but excluded from restaurant payout. The brief gives no formula, collector, or recipient, so this engine cannot calculate or allocate it reliably.
14. Dispute CSV contains one OPEN or CLOSE event per row. A CLOSE event applies only an explicit amount and supported refund reason; a resolution label alone never implies a refund. A positive adjustment is cumulative for the order only when its refund reason matches the existing reason.
15. Post-settlement dispute changes require `correct-settlement`. Corrections append a before/after statement revision and per-order audit rows; base statements and original audit rows remain immutable. Ads already charged are retained unless the revised payable amount is lower, in which case the difference becomes carried debit.
16. Corrections update the restaurant ad debit only when correcting its latest statement. Later statements and their carries are not automatically recalculated; correct affected later statements separately.
17. A single order cannot combine dispute refunds with different funders because the order refund schema has one reason field; such input is rejected instead of silently misallocating the refund.
18. Duplicate import detection is file-content based and namespaced by input type; duplicate order IDs in a different file fail atomically. Settled orders cannot be selected into another original settlement.
19. For a SPILLED refund, the refund is split to paise; when the amount has an odd number of paise, the platform share receives the extra paisa as explicitly stated by BR-11. The platform half is rounded half-up and the remainder is assigned to the restaurant.
20. CSV imports reject missing required fields, duplicate order IDs in an input batch, negative cancellation seconds, and `accepted` values other than 0 or 1. An omitted order outcome retains the documented DELIVERED default. No refund cap, delivery-fee allocation rule, or new tax rule is inferred.
21. Dispute OPEN timestamps must not precede the order timestamp, and CLOSE timestamps must not precede OPEN. Event batches are committed atomically; a failed row rolls the whole batch back. These are validation and ledger-consistency rules, not inferred refund outcomes.
22. Repeating a settlement correction that produces the same latest monetary statement does not append another revision. The first correction still records a revision and reason even if its values match, preserving the requested review action.
